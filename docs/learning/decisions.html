Decision Record

Why cassie-indexer looks the way it does. Each entry names the choice, the rejected alternative, and the reason.

North star

What counts as a trade idea

A trade idea is a post where the author expresses a directional view on a specific market subject, such that you could act on it.

Three simultaneous bars: a nameable subject, an assignable direction, and an expressible price consequence. The author's verbatim words are the evidence (frozen in quotes[]/headline_quote); our interpretation lives separately in thesis. One belief = one idea; instruments are expressions, not extra ideas. The sharpest exclusion: a view without a side, or a subject you can't name, is not a trade idea. Derived from paste.trade's thesis spec (SKILL.md §6 + sparse.md classifier), rewritten and adopted as this project's extraction gate.

Architecture

Two stages decoupled by the database

Chosen: collector (X → raw_posts) and processor (raw_posts → ideas) as separate runnable stages. Rejected: one monolithic hourly job that fetches and extracts inline. They run at different speeds and fail for different reasons; X quota is the scarce resource, so reprocessing must never require refetching. The DB is the queue: status='pending' is the work list.

Architecture

Fresh reimplementation; borrow only venue search concepts

This is a new codebase on purpose — not a fork of cassie. From cassie we mirrored the Hyperliquid /info request types (perpDexs, metaAndAssetCtxs, allMids) and the Polymarket Gamma approach, as plain-fetch adapters with no shared code. Execution stays in cassie entirely: the indexer holds no private keys (least privilege — it indexes, it does not trade).

Processor

Bounded routing, not an agentic tool-loop

Chosen: deterministic venue validation first, then one LLM ranking call over the validated list. Rejected: cassie's open tool-loop (assess_expression_fit → rank_expressions). At ~80 handles/hour unattended, the loop's cost and unpredictability outweigh its flexibility — and structurally preventing instrument hallucination matters more when no human reviews each result.

Processor

Gate folded into extraction; evidence enforced in code

One structured call returns is_idea:false or the ideas — no separate classifier round-trip (gpt-5.4-mini is cheap; most posts are noise and exit early). The verbatim-quote rule is enforced mechanically after the call: any idea whose quotes aren't substrings of the post is dropped. Prompts drift; String.includes doesn't.

Data sources

Polygon + CoinGecko + elbstream (paste.trade's verified stack)

Verified from paste.trade's exported data (4,733 trades using elbstream.com/logos/symbol, platform:"coingecko" records, Polygon in types.ts): equities → Polygon.io (the only realistic source of minute-level history for "price at post time"); crypto spot tail → CoinGecko; perps → Hyperliquid; predictions → Polymarket; logos → ticker-keyed elbstream URLs, cosmetic and lazy.

Pricing

Off-hours equity entry = last trade before the post

A 2am-Saturday $NVDA call gets the last minute-bar close before the post (7-day lookback), not Monday's open — the baseline is what the author was looking at. The gap is recorded in entry_note. Polymarket entries were the price at index time — Gamma only serves spot odds — which meant every reprocess reset the baseline to "now" and every PM card read 0.0% since posted. Fixed by reaching past Gamma to the CLOB /prices-history endpoint (pm.priceAt): it returns a per-token timeseries, so a prediction now gets its real odds at post time (resolve conditionId → yes CLOB token → nearest point in a ±6h window, signed for the held side). Falls back to the index-time price only when a market's history is unavailable (new/thin), still honestly noted.

Presentation

Two "why"s: the author's case vs the routing pipeline

The trader-facing reasoning is the author's investment case — setup (what happened / why it's mispriced) → edge (why the crowd is wrong) → payoff (target/upside + catalyst) — the argument you read to decide whether to take the position. That's a new reasoning field (trade_ideas), distilled by the extractor from the author's argument, and it headlines the detail view as "The case". It is not the same as pipeline, which traces how the system chose the instrument (candidate → venue-validated → picked). That routing derivation is audit plumbing — kept, but demoted to a collapsed block. Conflating the two (showing routing steps as the reasoning) was the original mistake; a trader needs the case for the trade, not a trace of our plumbing.

Presentation

context and pipeline: card fields at no extra cost

To make paste.trade-grade cards, two fields ride calls the processor already makes. context (trade_ideas, from the extract call) is paste.trade's ticker_context: 1–3 jargon-free sentences explaining the subject, no view. The extractor has OpenAI web search available for subject resolution, fresh context, and ticker discovery, but the trade-idea decision remains post-only: direction and evidence must come from the author's words. pipeline (routes, from the router's ranking call, replacing the old single rationale string) is the derivation chain — ordered steps[] each tagged basis: quote | market | inference, plus a one-line explanation. Naming caveat: pipeline the column (per-idea reasoning) collides with "the pipeline" the system flow — same word, different scope; kept per product naming.

Presentation

Every idea gets a full strategy; provenance tags keep it honest

User requirement: each trade idea should carry a complete strategy — exit plan, hold duration, stop loss, take profit. That collides with the earlier rule ("record only levels the author stated") because authors rarely give all four. Chosen: per-leg provenance. trade_ideas.strategy holds all four legs; each is tagged basis: "author" (their stated level/timing, close to their words) or basis: "suggested" (our disciplined, conservative gap-fill from conviction, horizon, and instrument volatility). The UI renders the tag on every leg, so a derived stop can never read as the author's plan. Rejected: leaving gaps blank (the user wants an actionable plan on every card) and silent synthesis (would put words in the author's mouth). New ideas get strategy at extract time; the existing corpus was backfilled by a per-idea call over stored fields — no re-extraction, no X quota.

Presentation

Trade params flex by instrument; stops are extracted, never invented

A trader clicking a card runs four questions in order: did I miss it? (the since-posted move — already loudest), who said it? (author track record), what's the argument? (the case), and what's the precise trade, and where am I wrong? The last one is instrument-shaped, so the detail view flexes. Price instruments (perps/stocks/spot) get a Trade plan — the author's target and invalidation, new trade_ideas columns the extractor fills only when the author explicitly stated a level (number, chart level, or magnitude); most posts state neither and show neither. Chosen: a missing stop over a fabricated one — an invented invalidation reads as the author's risk plan when it isn't. Predictions instead get a Resolution block (settle date + criteria) and an odds-framed header: a Gamma price of 0.62 is 62% implied probability, not "$0.62", so showing it as a dollar price was simply wrong. Resolution is fetched live (pm.marketInfo) so criteria never go stale; endDate stored at routing time is the fallback.

Presentation

Author track record → one win rate, direction-adjusted

The single tweet can't say whether the author is any good — the second question in a trader's head after "did I miss it?". Chosen: the detail view fires a second, non-blocking /api/authors/:handle request and renders a single win rate under the author's name (35% win rate on 17 scored calls), grouped with the source tweet so evidence and credibility read together. Its failure or slow live-price recompute is swallowed and never blanks the idea. Rejected: the first cut showed three stats (tracked · W/S up · avg %) — busier and, worse, wrong for shorts: the since-posted move was the raw price change, so a short that fell (a win) scored negative. Fix: sincePostedPct is now direction-adjusted (× −1 for short/no) at the card level, so the whole app — card colour, "since posted", win count, win rate — agrees that a correct short is a win. It's a mark-to-now rate (in profit vs. post-time price), not realized at a target/stop; that honesty is stated in the UI and position lifecycle stays parked.

Cost

Extractor model: settled on Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite (~15× cheaper than mini)

With X ingestion down to ~$8/mo, the LLM extraction became the dominant cost. We A/B'd three models on 10 real posts (scripts/ab-model.ts): gpt-5.4-mini ($0.75/$4.50 per 1M), gpt-5.4-nano ($0.20/$1.25, ~3.7× cheaper, stricter gate), and Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite ($0.10/$0.40, ~15× cheaper). Chosen: Gemini. It had the best cost-to-quality — strongest grounding specificity (real price levels and catalysts via Google Search), and the best gate calls on the two hardest posts: it nailed a dense $META research post that mini dropped and nano fumbled, and it was the only one to reject an untradeable tiny-college muni bond that mini/nano manufactured a trade from. Its risk is being more opinionated (flipped an ambiguous post bearish, split it into per-name shorts, occasionally adds comparison tickers). Provider abstraction: src/util/model.ts resolveModel() routes by model id (gemini→Google, gpt→OpenAI); the extractor branches its tools too — OpenAI's native web_search vs Gemini's Google Search grounding (which, notably, combines with structured output on 3.1-class models but not 2.5-lite). Switch is one env line (EXTRACTOR_MODEL). Known gap: Gemini's grounding citations don't yet populate the reference pills (different metadata shape than OpenAI sources).

Cost

Qwen pre-gates extraction and ranks validated routes

Chosen: qwen/qwen3.6-flash now makes the cheapest safe decisions at the edges of extraction. A high-recall multimodal pre-gate rejects only genuine non-opportunities before Gemini research runs; accepted posts retain the grounded extractor unchanged. Routing also moves to Qwen because it is a bounded JSON ranking task over instruments the venue adapters already proved exist. Both calls use OpenRouter's Chat Completions endpoint with reasoning disabled, JSON mode enabled, and Zod validation. Boundary: Qwen does not replace grounded extraction in this change, and a Qwen failure surfaces as a processing failure rather than silently changing model behavior.

Live 10-post replay: Qwen passed all ten historical A/B cases, including the tiny-college S&P relay that Gemini ultimately rejected. That means zero Qwen false negatives on the set, but also zero avoided Gemini/search calls; a separate obvious-noise fixture (“gm everyone, have a great weekend”) was rejected correctly. The full pipeline produced six ideas: four routed and two unrouted. Fresh Gemini extraction—not the Qwen gate—dropped the oil-collar, capex-research, and MU-watchlist posts that earlier generations accepted, because those rejections carried a null Qwen rejection reason. On the only clean stored-route comparison, both routers selected USO, but Qwen mislabeled the derived geopolitical mapping as direct. Conclusion: the gate currently protects recall but has not yet demonstrated material savings on hard financial posts, and route selection looks viable while provenance classification needs a larger evaluation.

Full-population validation: bun run evaluate:qwen-gate replayed every 595 eligible processed root posts with production thread assembly and images. One stale X image failed at the provider; of 594 completed calls, Qwen passed 484 and rejected 110, an 18.5% Gemini-call avoidance rate. Against currently persisted ideas it measured 128 true positives, 18 false negatives, 92 true negatives, and 356 passes on historical negatives (87.7% label recall). Those database labels are not clean ground truth: manual review found old fabricated ideas for smiley faces, jokes, and generic product chatter, while many “historical negatives” were obvious missed opportunities such as explicit $MSFT positioning, oil moves, earnings, upgrades, and Iran strikes. Human review of the 18 labeled misses found that most were correctly rejected noise, but several breaking Iran/NATO events were genuine misses under the opportunity-first policy. Conclusion: the gate yields material but modest savings; it needs a geopolitical-event recall adjustment before it is safe as the production boundary.

Prompt correction without example poisoning: the gate now asks only whether deeper financial analysis could reasonably uncover an opportunity; it does not receive asset mappings, event categories, or example trades. Replaying the same full population improved label recall from 87.7% to 89.0% (130/146) while increasing call avoidance from 18.5% to 19.0% (113/594). The prior Iran explosion and power-cut misses now pass without being taught an oil mapping. NATO news and a bare FX-options-expiry headline remain notable misses. This simpler decision boundary is both less biased and measurably better than enumerating what the model should consider actionable.

Balanced 100-post check: a random 50/50 sample of posts with and without currently persisted ideas produced 41 true positives, 9 labeled false negatives, 11 true negatives, and 39 labeled false positives: 82% raw label recall and 22% historical-negative rejection. Manual review showed why these are agreement metrics, not quality metrics. Seven of the nine “missed ideas” were junk labels (a smiley, generic AI chatter, political opinion, academic news, and a philosophical quote); the two credible lost opportunities were NATO news and an Iran explosion. Conversely, the historical-negative passes included explicit ticker positions, earnings, upgrades, commodity catalysts, and trade-policy shocks that the old extractor appears to have missed. The gate is behaving as intended—high recall and conservative rejection—but the stored extraction output is too noisy to serve as authoritative ground truth.

Platform

AI SDK upgraded v5 → v7

Moved the Vercel AI SDK from v5 to the current v7 (ai@7, @ai-sdk/openai@4, @ai-sdk/google@4 — the provider-spec-4 line is what v7 expects; a version mismatch here is what made an early @ai-sdk/google@4 install fail under v5). Breaking changes migrated: the structured-output option renamed experimental_output → output, and the result accessor result.experimental_output → result.output (Output is still exported, now an alias); generateObject was unchanged. Verified end-to-end (extract + route) live on v7.

Cost

Replaced the official X API with twitterapi.io (~33× cheaper)

The official X API pay-per-use is $0.005/post read. twitterapi.io (a third-party provider) is $0.00015/tweet — ~33× cheaper — and returns more: quoted and retweeted tweets arrive inlined (no expansion to buy, no lazy fetch), and inReplyToUserId gives self-reply detection in one field. Auth is a single x-api-key header. So the whole prior cost program (owned-reads, the expansion drop, the List) collapses into "point the collector here": collect.ts calls twitterapi.ts; the X client (twitter.ts) is retained as a fallback adapter. Endpoint choice matters enormously. The obvious /user/last_tweets has no since filter — only cursor + ~20/page — so every poll re-bills a full page and cost tracks frequency (~$40/mo adaptive, up to $173 hourly). Their docs literally warn against it for frequent polling. The right endpoint is /tweet/advanced_search: a query (from:a OR from:b …) since:<watermark> returns ONLY tweets newer than what we already have, batched ~20 authors/query (~4 queries for 80 handles). Cost then tracks new tweets (~36k/mo → ~$5–9/mo), and frequency is free again — poll every few minutes for near-real-time at the same price. The watermark is max(posted_at) minus a 15-min overlap (late-arrival safety; dedup on tweet_id makes the overlap free of dupes). Verified live: one cycle ingested 723 new posts across all 80 handles (84 quotes with inlined context, 32 self-replies, 0 retweets). Trade-off accepted: it's a scraping reseller (reliability/ToS risk), so the intended shape keeps X owned-read as a fallback floor. A WebSocket monitor (wss://ws.twitterapi.io) is the phase-2 upgrade — same ~$5/mo, sub-second push, but needs an always-on daemon.

Cost

Stop re-fetching tweets we already own; measure the rest

X API v2 bills on posts read, and our corpus showed the waste: of 743 pulled posts, 307 (41%) were replies-to-others we discard, and 404 carried a referenced_tweets expansion — an extra billed post-read each. The naive fix (exclude=replies) was rejected: it also kills self-threads, i.e. buying less data, not saving. Chosen instead: (1) drop the eager referenced_tweets expansion — a self-reply's parent is the same author's own tweet, which the timeline pull already returns, so expanding it re-fetches (and re-bills) tweets we own; self-threads are reassembled locally from raw_posts (thread.ts already did this). (2) isSelfReply from owned data — the parent id is free on the referenced_tweets field; it's a self-reply iff that parent is one of the author's own tweets (in this page or already stored), no expansion needed. (3) Lazy quoted-context — quoted-tweet text is fetched in the processor only for posts that reach extraction, not eagerly for all 743 (replies-to-others are skipped before that, so their parents are never fetched). (4) Measure — every collect cycle logs /2/usage/tweets (project posts used vs cap) so tuning is data-driven, not guessed. Legacy rows keep their inline referencedText, so reprocessing needs no re-fetch. Loses no coverage; the List-timeline consolidation (78 per-user pulls → one List pull) is tracked separately.

Discovered while building

Two live-API corrections

(1) HL stock perps are namespaced on builder dexes (xyz:INTC) — the universe map indexes bare symbols too, or equity tickers never match. (2) Gamma's /events?search= relevance is near-random (cassie compensated with an AI query planner); /public-search ranks well and needs no planner. Both found by hitting the real APIs during the smoke run, not by reading docs.

Output

Serve first (read API), render second (Svelte feed)

Chosen: a read-only Hono API over the existing tables, then a Svelte 5 SPA on top. Rejected: paste.trade's push + live-stream model (per-source pages updated in real time as an agent works). That model exists because a human triggers each ingest and watches; our indexer is autonomous and unattended, so there's nothing to watch live — a pull API over batch-written rows fits. The API reads stored idea, route, and entry-baseline rows, then fetches current price live from the selected venue before serializing the card. Two mockup fields we lack data for (+% today, "you follow traded this") are replaced with real fields (entry reference; conviction/horizon) rather than fabricated — see the feed page.

Ingestion

Thread reassembly — extract a self-thread once, not per tweet

In a self-thread each tweet is also the author's own timeline post, so every tweet became its own row and got extracted independently — a single thesis produced duplicates (parent + child) and orphan fragments (a child like "API tokens" makes no sense alone). Fix: the collector stores reply_to_tweet_id; thread.ts reassembles the same-author chain (walk to root, BFS descendants); the processor extracts the assembled text once, anchored to the root (its posted_at is the entry-price baseline, its tweet is the card link). Made idempotent by deleting prior ideas for all members first, which also handles a thread growing across hourly runs. Verified on real data: a 7-tweet CapitalShipyard thread collapsed from 2 fragments to 1 idea; ~97 thread-member posts across threads up to 9 tweets reduced to one extraction each. Known edge: two very long threads (9 + tweets) consistently returned off-schema from gpt-5.4-mini and are marked failed.

Ingestion

Process only self-threads; skip replies to other people

Replies were ~28% of routed cards, and about half carried a genuine take — but the other half were reactive noise (a "100%" agreement → COIN short; a "don't fullport, it's a meme" warning → HOOD long). Decision: accept the precision-for-recall trade and drop replies-to-others entirely, keeping originals, quotes, and self-threads. The enabler is is_self_reply — set when the tweet's author_id matches the replied-to tweet's — which distinguishes a self-thread (author continuing their own tweet) from a conversation with someone else. The processor skips any thread whose root is a reply-to-others, with no LLM call. This purged 19 routed cards from the live feed (COIN/HOOD/MRAM et al), 70 → 51. Blunt alternative rejected: X's exclude=replies also kills self-threads; the flag is what makes the cut surgical. (Existing rows predate the flag and read false, so the one-time purge treated all replies as reply-to-others; new sweeps set it precisely, keeping self-threads.)
